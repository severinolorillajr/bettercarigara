# Get free check-ups, basic medicines and vaccines

Primary health care in Carigara is delivered by the **Municipal Health Office**
and the barangay health stations. Health services were devolved to local
governments by the Local Government Code, so these are run by the Municipality
of Carigara, not by the national Department of Health.

A consultation is **free**. Everything below about Carigara comes from the
municipality's own **Citizen's Charter 2026**.

## What to bring

- A **referral slip** from your barangay health station — you get this in step 1
  below
- A **valid ID** — PhilHealth, SSS, GSIS, BIR, or another government-issued ID
- Your **MDR, 4Ps or PhilHealth card**, if you have one
- **Laboratory results**, if the doctor has asked for them

## How a consultation works

| You                                                      | What happens                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Go to your barangay health station                    | The midwife records your history and vital signs and gives you a referral slip — 15 minutes |
| 2. Take the referral slip to the Municipal Health Office | The midwife or nurse on duty interviews you and takes your vital signs again — 3 minutes    |
| 3. See the Municipal Health Officer                      | Physical examination; laboratory tests are requested if needed — 10 to 15 minutes           |
| 4. Bring back any laboratory results                     | The doctor makes a final assessment and prescribes treatment — about 5 minutes              |
| 5. Return to the midwife with your referral slip         | You receive the **available** medicines and instructions for a follow-up — 5 minutes        |

**Emergencies are not handled this way.** The charter says an emergency is
referred immediately to the hospital.

The charter's own wording is "dispense available medicines". A prescription
from the Health Office is not a promise that the medicine is in stock.

## Register for PhilHealth YAKAP

This is the single most useful thing most residents can do, and it is free.

**YAKAP** — _Yaman ng Kalusugan Program_ — is PhilHealth's primary care benefit
package. It was launched on **25 July 2025**, replacing and expanding the earlier
Konsulta programme. If you have read older guidance referring to "Konsulta", this
is what it became.

Once you register and pick an accredited provider, the package covers:

- **Consultations** with your chosen provider
- **Medicines** — 75 listed drugs, up from 21 under Konsulta
- **Laboratory and screening tests** — 19, up from 13, now including screening
  for breast, lung, liver and colorectal cancer

**How to register:** through the PhilHealth member portal at
[philhealth.gov.ph](https://www.philhealth.gov.ph/yakap/), or in person at a
PhilHealth office with a valid ID. Registration means choosing the accredited
facility you will use.

You must be registered **before** you need care. Registering at the point of
illness is the most common reason people miss out on benefits they were already
entitled to.

## What is not published yet

_None of the following is verified for Carigara._

- Whether the Municipal Health Office is an accredited YAKAP provider
- Opening hours, and which days a physician is on duty — the charter does not
  state them
- The list of barangay health stations and their schedules across the 49
  barangays
- Which medicines are actually in stock

Vaccination is covered in the charter as its own service, and will get its own
page under maternal care and child immunization.

If you have used the Health Office recently, you know more than this page does.
See the repository README — corrections are the most useful contribution this
site takes.
