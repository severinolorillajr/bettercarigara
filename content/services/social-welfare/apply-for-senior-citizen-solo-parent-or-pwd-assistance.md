# Apply for a senior citizen, solo parent or PWD ID

The Municipal Social Welfare and Development Office (MSWDO) issues the **PWD
ID and purchase booklet**, the **senior citizen ID and purchase booklet** (from
age **60**), and the **solo parent ID**. All three are **free** and are
released the same day — in under **an hour**, by the charter's count. The MSWDO
also takes applications for the national **cash gift** for residents turning
**80, 85, 90, 95 and 100**.

Everything below about Carigara comes from the municipality's own **Citizen's
Charter 2026**.

(PDAO)** within the MSWDO. Senior citizen services are handled with the

Go **in person** — every service on this page starts with you appearing at the
office.

## PWD ID and purchase booklet

The ID and booklet give the benefits of **RA 9442**, as the charter lists them:
free medical and dental diagnostic and laboratory services in government
facilities, and **20% discounts** on medicines, hotels, restaurants,
recreation centres, theatres, cinemas and concert halls, and domestic air, sea
and public transport fares.

**Who can apply:** registered persons with disability who live in Carigara.
The charter lists these types of disability, from the Philippine Registry for
PWDs:

- Deaf or hard of hearing
- Intellectual disability
- Learning disability
- Mental disability
- Physical disability
- Psychosocial disability
- Speech and language impairment
- Visual disability
- Cancer (RA 11515)
- Rare disease (RA 10747)

### Two entries, one ID

The charter describes this ID **twice**:

- p. 274, "Application of PWD identification cards and purchase booklets",
  handled by the **PDAO**. You submit your documents, the office **registers
  you in the Philippine Registry for PWDs**, and you leave with the ID and
  booklet — **50 minutes**.
- p. 281, "Issuance of person with disability card", handled by the **MSWDO**.
  You are interviewed, your documents are reviewed, the ID and booklet are
  printed and released — **45 minutes**.

Both end with the same ID and booklet released to you. The charter does not
say how the two relate. The first is the only one that covers renewals and
replacements and asks for a Certificate of Disability, so this page follows
it. The second entry's requirements (below) look copied from the senior
citizen ID: it asks for a "Senior Citizen Form" and says it is for "indigent
senior citizen" residents. Ask the office which list applies to you.

### What to bring — new ID

| Document                                                                  | Where to get it                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Filled-out application form                                               | PDAO                                                                                                                      |
| **Two** 1×1 ID pictures                                                   | You                                                                                                                       |
| Recent **Certificate of Disability**, stating the disability              | A registered doctor specialising in the disability, or the Municipal Health Officer (for an **apparent** disability only) |
| Barangay clearance — for a minor, in the parent's or guardian's name      | Your Punong Barangay's office                                                                                             |
| Blood type (optional)                                                     | You                                                                                                                       |
| Affidavit of guardianship — if the contact person is not immediate family | You                                                                                                                       |

If the disability is apparent, the person with disability should come in
person.

### Renewal, lost, damaged or wrong ID

Bring the application form, **two** 1×1 pictures and a barangay clearance
(in the parent's or guardian's name for a minor), plus:

| Situation          | Also bring                                                      |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Renewal            | An **updated** Certificate of Disability; blood type (optional) |
| Lost ID            | A **notarised** affidavit of loss                               |
| Damaged ID         | A **notarised** affidavit of mutilation                         |
| An error on the ID | A **notarised** affidavit of discrepancy                        |

### Steps (PDAO, p. 274)

| You                           | The office                                                                                       | Time       |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| 1. Submit your requirements   | Checks and approves them, encodes you in the Philippine Registry for PWDs, processes the request | 35 minutes |
| 2. Receive the ID and booklet | Gives a short orientation on using the ID, then releases the ID and booklet                      | 15 minutes |

**Total: 50 minutes.**

### The p. 281 version

It asks for: a **barangay residency** certificate showing age and complete
address, a **medical certificate** stating the type of disability, **two** 1×1
and **one** 2×2 pictures, and the filled-out form from the MSWDO. Steps:
interview (10 minutes), document review (10 minutes), printing (20 minutes),
release and orientation on benefits (5 minutes) — **45 minutes** in all.

## Senior citizen ID and purchase booklet

For residents aged **60 and above**. The ID and booklet give the benefits of
**RA 9994**, as the charter lists them: free medical and dental diagnostic and
laboratory services in government facilities, and **20% discounts** on
medicines, hotels, restaurants, recreation centres, theatres, cinemas and
concert halls, and domestic air, sea and public transport fares.

The charter's eligibility line reads "Bonafide **Indigent** Senior Citizen
Residents of Carigara, Leyte, ages 60 and above". It does not say how
indigency is checked or whether non-indigent seniors are turned away; ask the
office.

### What to bring

| Document                                                                 | Where to get it                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Barangay residency certificate showing your **age** and complete address | Your barangay hall              |
| **Two** 1×1 and **one** 2×2 pictures                                     | A photo studio or rush ID store |
| Filled-out senior citizen form                                           | MSWDO                           |

### Steps

| You                                  | The office                                           | Time       |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1. Come in person and ask for the ID | The OSCA head or federation president interviews you | 10 minutes |
| 2. Present your documents            | OSCA reviews them                                    | 10 minutes |
| 3. Claim the ID                      | Releases it and explains your benefits               | 15 minutes |

**Total: 35 minutes.**

## Cash gift for octogenarians, nonagenarians and centenarians

Under **RA 11982**, approved on **26 February 2024**, Filipinos reaching
**80, 85, 90 and 95** receive a cash gift — at home or abroad. The charter
also lists **100-year-olds** as eligible.

The charter names two amounts, ₱10,000 and ₱100,000, but does not say which
age gets which. It also prints the second as "One Hundred Thousand Pesos
(10,000.00)" — the words and the figure disagree.

### What to bring

| Document                                                                    | Where to get it                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Application form                                                            | MSWDO or OSCA                                                      |
| PSA birth certificate                                                       | Philippine Statistics Authority                                    |
| Endorsement letter                                                          | MSWDO or the Mayor's Office; the consulate or DFA if living abroad |
| A recent 2×2 picture, and a **full-body** picture printed on A4 photo paper | You                                                                |

**If the beneficiary has died**, the claimant also brings:

- The original death certificate
- A photocopy of a valid government ID of the nearest surviving relative
- A photocopy of proof of relationship to the deceased
- A judicial or extrajudicial settlement, if there is more than one claimant

### Steps

| You                                               | The office                                                     | Time       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1. Submit the form and documents to OSCA or MSWDO | Reviews them, interviews you and endorses you as a beneficiary | 33 minutes |
| 2. Wait for the pay-out schedule                  | MSWDO or OSCA tells you the date                               | —          |
| 3. Receive the cash gift                          | The NCSC pays out                                              | 15 minutes |

**Total at the counter: 48 minutes**, plus the wait for the pay-out date. The
charter does not say how long that wait is.

## Solo parent ID

The ID lets a qualified solo parent claim the benefits of the solo parents law.
The charter cites **RA 8972**, and the requirements include an orientation on
**RA 11861**. The ID is issued on the recommendation of the municipal social
worker.

**Who qualifies** — a Carigara resident who is:

- A woman who gave birth as a result of rape or another crime against chastity,
  and keeps and raises the child
- A parent left solo because the spouse **died**
- A parent left solo because the spouse is **detained or serving a sentence**
  for at least **one year**
- A parent left solo because the spouse is physically or mentally
  **incapacitated**, as certified by a public medical practitioner
- A parent left solo by **legal separation** or **annulment**, with custody of
  the children
- A parent **abandoned** by the spouse for at least **one year**
- An unmarried mother or father who keeps and raises the child
- Anyone else who alone provides parental care and support to a child
- Any family member who takes on the responsibility of head of the family

### What to bring

The charter lists all of these. The last four apply only in the situation
noted.

| Document                                                           | Where to get it                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Intake sheet                                                       | MSWDO                                   |
| Application for solo parent ID                                     | MSWDO                                   |
| CENOMAR or marriage certificate                                    | PSA or the Municipal Civil Registrar    |
| Affidavit of two disinterested persons                             | Public Attorney's Office (PAO)          |
| Affidavit of tax exemption                                         | BIR                                     |
| Barangay certificate as solo parent, **stating the reason**        | Your barangay hall or barangay chairman |
| **Two** 1×1 and **one** 2×2 pictures                               | A photo studio or rush ID store         |
| Photocopy of the birth certificates of your children aged **0–22** | Municipal Civil Registrar               |
| Orientation on RA 11861                                            | MSWDO                                   |
| Partner's death certificate — if the partner died                  | You, or the Civil Registrar             |
| Certificate of detention — if the partner is detained              | Bureau of Jail Management and Penology  |
| Partner's medical certificate — if the partner is bedridden        | A doctor                                |
| POEA standard employment contract — if the partner is an OFW       | POEA                                    |

The charter lists an OFW partner's contract without listing an OFW partner
among the categories above.

### Steps

| You                                  | The office                                   | Time       |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1. Come in person and ask for the ID | Interviews and assesses you                  | 10 minutes |
| 2. Present your documents            | Reviews them and prepares the ID for signing | 20 minutes |
| 3. Claim the ID                      | Releases it and explains your benefits       | 25 minutes |

**Total: 55 minutes.**
