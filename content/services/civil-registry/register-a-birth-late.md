# Register a birth late

If a birth was not registered within 30 days, it can still be registered — this
is called **delayed** or **late registration**. It needs more documents than an
ordinary registration, a **10-day public posting**, and costs **₱150 to ₱300**.
There is also a **free** route for indigent families.

Everything below about Carigara comes from the municipality's own **Citizen's
Charter 2026**.

**Who can apply:** the person themselves if of age; the parents or a guardian
for a minor; or the nearest relative if the person has died.

## Which route applies to you

| Situation                                                   | Fee      | How long                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Aged **79 or younger**, parents married                     | ₱150.00  | 11 days, plus about 2 hours         |
| Aged **79 or younger**, parents not married                 | ₱300.00  | 11 days, plus about 2 hours         |
| Aged **80 or older**                                        | ₱150.00  | **About 3 months** — see below      |
| Born **somewhere else**, registering in Carigara            | ₱250.00  | 12 days, then forwarded — see below |
| Indigent family — **Birth Registration Assistance Project** | **Free** | Up to 6 months                      |

## What to bring

Bring **3 photocopies** of each document.

**Everyone needs:**

| Document                                                                                                                                 | Where to get it                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Certificate of Live Birth, 4 copies, filled out and signed                                                                               | Municipal Health Office, hospital, birthing clinic or the Civil Registrar |
| Affidavit of delayed registration                                                                                                        | Notary public                                                             |
| Negative certification — proof PSA has no record of the birth                                                                            | Philippine Statistics Authority                                           |
| **Any two** of: baptismal certificate, school records, income tax return, insurance policy, medical records, voter's registration record | The church, school, BIR, insurer, clinic or COMELEC                       |
| Affidavit of two disinterested persons                                                                                                   | Notary public                                                             |
| National ID, ePhilID, digital ID or validated transaction slip (not needed for a child under 1)                                          | PhilSys registration centre                                               |
| Barangay certification as proof of residency                                                                                             | Your barangay hall                                                        |
| Unedited front-facing 2×2 photo                                                                                                          | —                                                                         |
| Personal appearance                                                                                                                      | —                                                                         |

**Depending on your case:**

- Certificate of marriage, if married
- Birth certificate of the parents
- Affidavit on the whereabouts of the mother, if she is not available
- For a child whose parents are not married: affidavit of admission of
  paternity, affidavit to use the surname of the father, or — if born before
  3 August 1988 — affidavit of acknowledgement of paternity
- If a parent is foreign: valid passport, BI clearance, and ACR I-Card
- If the person has died: their death certificate and an affidavit that they
  are deceased
- For a birth that happened elsewhere: an out-of-town registration affidavit
  and an affidavit of corroboration

The **affidavit of two disinterested persons** can also be done as a **joint
affidavit at the Mayor's Office**: bring a valid ID and the two witnesses in
person.

## Steps

| You                                                 | The office                                                                           | Time       |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| 1. Bring all the documents to the Civil Registrar   | Checks them and fills out the Certificate of Live Birth                              | 45 minutes |
| 2. Review the form carefully and sign it            | Prepares the registrar's affidavit                                                   | 15 minutes |
| 3. Get an order of payment and pay at the Treasurer | Issues the official receipt                                                          | 25 minutes |
| 4. Return with the receipt                          | Posts a **notice of delayed registration** on the LGU bulletin board for **10 days** | 10 days    |
| 5. Come back on the 11th day                        | Assigns a registry number and signs the certificate — even if you are not there      | 20 minutes |
| 6. Collect your copy                                | Sends PSA its copy by the 10th of the following month                                | 10 minutes |

### If you are 80 or older

Instead of registering on the 11th day, the Civil Registrar sends your
documents to PSA to verify the date of birth, which the charter estimates at
**about 3 months**. This check exists because Republic Act 11982 gives a
**₱10,000 cash gift** at ages 80, 85, 90 and 95, and the date of birth decides
who receives it.

### If you were born somewhere else

Carigara does not register the birth itself. After the 10-day posting, the
documents go through PSA to the civil registrar of the place of birth, which
registers it there. The charter says it cannot give a date for this, because it
depends on PSA, and that the place of birth may charge its own fees.

## Free: the Birth Registration Assistance Project

For **indigenous peoples, Muslim Filipinos and the poorest households**.
Registration is free and done by a **mobile registration team** visiting the
barangay.

1. **Register at your barangay hall.** The barangay sends the list to the Civil
   Registrar, which asks PSA to confirm there is no existing record. The charter
   allows **up to 6 months** for this.
2. **When the team visits**, bring: the PSA negative certification, the
   affidavit of delayed registration (on the back of the birth certificate), an
   affidavit of two disinterested persons, a **certificate of indigency from the
   Punong Barangay**, any proof of identity, your national ID, and a 2×2 photo.
3. **Check the certificate and sign.** You receive your copy on the spot.
