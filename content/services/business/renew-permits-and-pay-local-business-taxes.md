# Renew permits and pay local business taxes

Business permits run on the calendar year. Renewal and the local business tax
are due **on or before 20 January**, and in Carigara the renewal is computed
from your **gross receipts** for the previous year.

Everything below about Carigara comes from the municipality's own **Citizen's
Charter 2026**. The deadline and extension rules come from the Local Government
Code.

---

## The deadline

|                         |                                          |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Due                     | On or before **20 January**              |
| Or, if paying quarterly | Within the first 20 days of each quarter |
| Legal basis             | Local Government Code, Section 167       |

The provision reads:

> "Unless otherwise provided in this Code, all local taxes, fees, and charges
> shall be paid within the first twenty (20) days of January or of each
> subsequent quarter, as the case may be."

**You may pay quarterly rather than annually.** Many businesses do not realise
this. The same twenty-day rule then applies at the start of each quarter.

## What to bring

Online, you upload each one; at the counter you hand over the originals, except
where a photocopy is noted. Unlike a new application, renewal does **not** ask
for an occupancy permit, but it does ask for proof of what you earned.

| Document                                                                 | Where to get it                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unified Business Permit Application Form                                 | Online after registering on the iBPLS portal, or at the BOSS area                                                             |
| Approved barangay clearance                                              | The barangay where the business operates                                                                                      |
| Community Tax Certificate (cedula)                                       | Municipal Treasurer's Office                                                                                                  |
| Proof of business registration                                           | DTI (sole proprietors), SEC (corporations and partnerships), CDA (cooperatives)                                               |
| BIR Certificate of Registration                                          | Bureau of Internal Revenue                                                                                                    |
| Contract of lease — or affidavit of no rental, or the owner's consent    | Your landlord or the property owner. If you own the property, its tax declaration                                             |
| Latest real property tax payment for the business location (photocopy)   | Municipal Treasurer's Office                                                                                                  |
| Real property tax declaration (photocopy)                                | Municipal Assessor's Office — see [Get or update a tax declaration](/services/taxes-property/get-or-update-a-tax-declaration) |
| **Basis for computing the tax** — one of the three below                 | See below                                                                                                                     |
| Market clearance — **public market vendors only**                        | Office of Market and Slaughter                                                                                                |
| Special power of attorney, notarised — **only if you are not the owner** | You                                                                                                                           |
| Government-issued photo ID (photocopy)                                   | With an SPA, bring IDs for both the owner and the representative                                                              |

The basis for computing the tax is one of:

- your **income tax return** — annual or quarterly; if quarterly, submit only
  the 1st, 2nd and 3rd quarters
- a **notarised sworn statement of gross sales**
- a **certificate of tax exemption**, if you are exempt

## Steps

| You                                | The office                                                                                                | Time       |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1. File online or at the BOSS area | BPLO checks your documents and sends the application to the offices that must sign off                    | 20 minutes |
| 2. Get your assessment             | The Treasurer's Office (Window 8) computes your tax and fees and gives you a **Tax Order of Payment**     | 15 minutes |
| 3. Pay and claim                   | The Treasurer's cashier issues an official receipt. Take it back to BPLO, which issues the renewed permit | 15 minutes |

About **50 minutes** at the counters, by the charter's count. Carigara
classifies this as a "Simple" transaction, so the legal maximum is **3 working
days**.

## Fees

Renewal carries the same fee list as a new permit — a mayor's permit fee that
depends on your line of business, an occupation fee that depends on your
number of employees, and fixed fees that add up to **₱1,920.00**, including a
₱1,200.00 garbage fee. The full itemised list is on
[Apply for a barangay clearance and a mayor's business permit](/services/business/apply-for-barangay-clearance-and-mayors-business-permits).
The business tax itself is computed from your gross receipts.

## If you cannot pay on time

The Sanggunian may extend the deadline **without surcharge or penalty**, for a
justifiable reason, for up to **six months**. That is a decision of the
Sangguniang Bayan, not something a counter clerk can grant — but it exists, and
it is worth asking about rather than assuming penalties are automatic.

If the deadline passes without an extension, the Sanggunian may impose a
surcharge and interest on the unpaid amount under Section 168.

## Late renewal

A permit that is not renewed does not quietly lapse — operating without a
current permit exposes the business to penalties and closure orders separate
from the tax surcharge. If you have missed the window, renewing late is better
than not renewing.

## Closing a business

If you are stopping rather than renewing, file a **business retirement**
through the same iBPLS portal or BOSS counter, so that taxes stop being
assessed. You will need:

- your latest business permit
- a retirement letter
- the basis for computing the tax, as above
- a market clearance, if you are a public market vendor
- a notarised SPA, if you are not the owner

The charter lists a ₱90.00 fee under the heading "Business Tax Certification
Fee", and the same three steps and 50 minutes as renewal.
