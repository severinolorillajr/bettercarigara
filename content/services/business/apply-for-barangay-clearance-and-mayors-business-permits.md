# Apply for a barangay clearance and a mayor's business permit

To operate a business in Carigara you need two things, in this order: a
**barangay clearance** from the barangay where the business is located, then a
**mayor's business permit** from the Business One Stop Shop (BOSS) at the
Municipal Hall — or online.

Everything below about Carigara comes from the municipality's own **Citizen's
Charter 2026**. The national rules come from the Local Government Code and the
Ease of Doing Business Act.

## Step 1 — Barangay clearance

Get this from the barangay where the business will operate. It comes first: the
municipality cannot legally issue your permit without it.

|             |                                                  |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Where       | The barangay hall covering your business address |
| Legal basis | Local Government Code, Section 152(c)            |
| Fee         | Set by your barangay's own ordinance             |
| How long    | **Seven working days** from filing               |

The law is unusually direct about the deadline:

> "The application for clearance shall be acted upon within seven (7) working
> days from the filing thereof. In the event that the clearance is not issued
> within the said period, the city or municipality may issue the said license or
> permit."

So if your barangay has not acted after seven working days, that is not a dead
end — the municipality may proceed without the clearance. Keep your filing
receipt, since it is what proves when the clock started.

## Step 2 — Gather the documents

For a **new** business. Online, you upload each one; at the BOSS counter you
hand over the originals, except where a photocopy is noted.

| Document                                                                 | Where to get it                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unified Business Permit Application Form                                 | Online after registering on the iBPLS portal, or at the BOSS area                                                             |
| Approved barangay clearance                                              | Your barangay (Step 1)                                                                                                        |
| Occupancy permit                                                         | Office of the Building Official                                                                                               |
| Community Tax Certificate (cedula)                                       | Municipal Treasurer's Office                                                                                                  |
| Proof of business registration                                           | DTI (sole proprietors), SEC (corporations and partnerships), CDA (cooperatives)                                               |
| BIR Certificate of Registration                                          | Bureau of Internal Revenue                                                                                                    |
| Contract of lease — or affidavit of no rental, or the owner's consent    | Your landlord or the property owner. If you own the property, its tax declaration                                             |
| Latest real property tax payment for the business location (photocopy)   | Municipal Treasurer's Office                                                                                                  |
| Real property tax declaration (photocopy)                                | Municipal Assessor's Office — see [Get or update a tax declaration](/services/taxes-property/get-or-update-a-tax-declaration) |
| Market clearance — **public market vendors only**                        | Office of Market and Slaughter                                                                                                |
| Special power of attorney, notarised — **only if you are not the owner** | You                                                                                                                           |
| Government-issued photo ID (photocopy)                                   | With an SPA, bring IDs for both the owner and the representative                                                              |

**If you sell food**, you will also need a **sanitary permit** from the
Municipal Health Office. Food establishments need a stool exam, HBsAg test and
sputum exam; hospitality workers need a personal hygiene test. A new
establishment gets an on-site inspection by the sanitary inspector; if anything
needs fixing, the re-inspection comes **after 7 working days**.

## Step 3 — File, get assessed, pay

| You                                | The office                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Time       |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| 1. File online or at the BOSS area | BPLO checks your documents and sends the application to the offices that must sign off: planning and zoning, the Building Official, the police, the sanitary inspector, the Treasurer, the Bureau of Fire Protection and, for vendors, the market supervisor | 20 minutes |
| 2. Get your assessment             | The Treasurer's Office (Window 8) computes your taxes and fees and gives you a **Tax Order of Payment**                                                                                                                                                      | 15 minutes |
| 3. Pay and claim                   | The Treasurer's cashier issues an official receipt. Take it back to BPLO, which issues the permit and clearances                                                                                                                                             | 15 minutes |

If any of the signing-off offices finds something missing, you must comply
before moving on.

**Expect a site visit.** The zoning check is done by the Municipal Planning and
Development Office within **one working day** of BPLO forwarding your
application, and the charter says a site inspection is "usually required" for
new businesses.

The fixed amounts add up to **₱1,920.00**. On top of that come the mayor's
permit fee, the occupation fee and the business tax, which the Treasurer

reproduced as the charter prints them.

These are the amounts the charter states. The rates themselves are set by the
municipal revenue code, which has not been retrieved.

## Small retailers: the simpler mayor's permit

The charter lists a separate, walk-in-only **mayor's permit** for "Small Retail
Owners; Business Owners earning gross monthly income of Php 30,000.00". It does
not say whether that figure is a ceiling — ask BPLO whether you qualify.

It needs fewer documents: the application form, barangay clearance, and
photocopies of your cedula, the latest real property tax payment for the
location, a market clearance (vendors only), an SPA (if not the owner) and a
government ID.

| Fee                | Amount                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Mayor's permit fee | Depends on the line of business |
| Garbage fee        | ₱1,200.00                       |
| Secretary's fee    | ₱50.00                          |
| Occupation fee     | ₱100.00 per person              |
| Health certificate | ₱50.00 per person               |
| Sanitary fee       | ₱200.00                         |

## If it takes too long

Under the Ease of Doing Business Act, an LGU's processing time cannot exceed:

| Transaction type                                              | Maximum         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Simple — which is how Carigara classifies business permits    | 3 working days  |
| Complex                                                       | 7 working days  |
| Highly technical, or posing danger to public health or safety | 20 working days |

If the office fails to act within its published time without due cause, the law
provides that the application is deemed approved, provided you submitted
everything required. Ask for your application to be officially received and
dated — that receipt is what makes the rule usable.

To complain, the charter directs you to the Public Assistance and Complaint
Desk at the Municipal Hall, to carigaralgu@yahoo.com, or to 0912 460 9390.
